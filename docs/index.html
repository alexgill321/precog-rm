Divergent Paths: Learning an Outcome-Predictive Representation of LLM Agent Trajectories

Alexander Gill1,2, Guram Gogia1, Francisco Guzmán1  ·  1Handshake AI, 2University of Utah

Paper Code Data & checkpoints

An agentic rollout is a path through a shared embedding space of actions and environment observations. Successful and failed attempts at the same task separate in that space, and simple per-task discriminators recover the outcome at up to 0.95 AUROC. Precog-RM meta-learns a representation in which that signal carries to unseen tasks and policies from a single batch of labeled rollouts, predicts outcomes mid-rollout, and, deployed as an early-stopping controller for GRPO, cuts training FLOPs by up to 47.3% at near-baseline pass@1.

Chapter 1 · Section 2 and Figure 1

Rollouts are paths through behavior states

Each step of an agentic rollout is one action and the observation it provokes. Embed both with a frozen text encoder and a rollout becomes a path through one shared action–observation space; the same task attempted fifty times gives fifty paths from the same start. Clustering the steps of one task (HDBSCAN over the joint action–observation similarity, minimum cluster size 5, unassigned steps in one outlier bin) turns those paths into routes through a small set of behavior states. Bar height is how many rollouts sit in a state at that step, bar colour is that state's empirical success rate, and each ribbon is one rollout coloured by its final outcome. The routes split early: some states are visited almost only by rollouts that go on to fail.

Try: click the deep-red bar around step 13 and read the action–observation pairs that put a rollout there; hover a ribbon to follow one attempt end to end.

Chapter 2 · Section 4.2, Table 1, Appendix A

The outcome is already in the path

If success and failure separate in this space, a classifier fit on a task's own rollouts should recover the outcome of rollouts it has not seen. It does. Within each task, stratified 3-fold cross-validation fits a nearest-centroid or RBF discriminator on the pooled representation, or a fastText model on the state-ID sequence, using about 33 labeled rollouts, and scores the held-out third. Across three benchmarks and three policy models the best per-task discriminator reaches 0.77 to 0.95 AUROC. The ceiling moves by about 0.12 across benchmarks and 0.05 across policies, with AgentClinic the consistent low performer. Two limits motivate the rest of the paper: a per-task fit transfers nothing to the next task, and its accuracy is capped by the labeled rollouts a single task provides.

Chapter 3 · Section 3, Figure 2

Learn the space instead of fitting each task

Precog-RM is a small transformer that maps a rollout, or any prefix of it, to a 128-d latent vector: each action–observation pair is embedded and linearly projected, a two-layer encoder reads the sequence, and masked mean pooling gives one vector z. There is no classifier head. On a new task a handful of finished rollouts with known outcomes form the support set; the mean latent of its passes and of its failures are the two prototypes, and P(pass) is a softmax over the negative distances from z to each. Training is episodic over many tasks: each episode draws a support set of eight passes and eight failures, encodes queries truncated to random prefixes, and minimises the prototype-distance loss, so the encoder learns to place trajectories by where they are headed rather than by what they look like.

Chapter 4 · Sections 4.3 and 4.4, Figure 3, Table 2

Few shots are enough, and it carries to new tasks and policies

Deployment offers far less than a full support set. With k labeled rollouts per class drawn from the training fold, Precog-RM beats the strongest per-task baseline at every k on every benchmark, by 0.06 to 0.12 AUROC at k=1, and at k=2 it already matches the baselines at k=8: a four-fold reduction in the labeled rollouts required. The learned space also transfers. An encoder meta-trained on one policy's rollouts still separates rollouts of a policy it never saw, and pooling all three policies gives the best or near-best column everywhere; across benchmarks transfer is partial, and recovers when the target benchmark joins the pool.

Try: drag k. The support set in chapter 3 shrinks to match, so you can see what two labeled rollouts per class look like on the map.

Chapter 5 · Section 4.5, Figure 4

It can tell before the end

An encoder trained only on complete rollouts is at chance on prefixes. Training with queries truncated to random lengths fixes that: the prefix-trained encoder maps a partial trajectory into the same space as full ones and its P(pass) becomes informative long before the rollout ends. Gated on confidence, the prediction turns into a decision: stop when P(pass) rises above θ or falls below 1−θ, otherwise keep going. Sweeping θ trades the accuracy of the stopped rollouts against the generation steps saved; on Tau2-Bench and BFCLv3 θ=0.9 stops 45% and 37% of rollouts at 0.84 and 0.90 accuracy, and on AgentClinic, where the signal is weak, the model mostly abstains.

Try: drag the prefix slider to watch the selected rollout's point travel; then move θ and see how many of this task's rollouts the controller would stop, how early, and how often it would be right.

Chapter 6 · Section 5, Table 3, Figure 5

Inside GRPO: stop the rollout, keep the optimizer

GRPO samples a group of sixteen rollouts per task and the generation is the compute bottleneck. The controller sits in rollout collection only: after each step it re-scores the prefix against the task's prototypes, and a rollout that crosses θ is terminated with the predicted outcome as its terminal reward. The objective, advantages and clipping are untouched. Support comes either from the run itself as rollouts finish (rolling) or from a pre-collected batch generated by a different policy (permanent), which exploits the cross-model transfer of chapter 4 and saves from the first step. Training Qwen3-8B on Tau2-Bench retail for 50 batches, the most conservative setting slightly exceeds baseline pass@1 at 10% less compute, and permanent support at θ=0.8 cuts training FLOPs by 47.3% while retaining 78% of the post-training gain. Only the loosest setting, θ=0.7 with no forced completions, crosses into degraded learning.
