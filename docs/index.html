Divergent Paths

Learning an outcome-predictive representation of LLM agent trajectories

One attempt.

An agent works through a task, action by action, observation by observation. It fails.

Rewind. Choose differently at one step.

Same states up to that step, a different action, and the outcome flips. That step is a critical branch point.

Two real rollouts of the task that share their first states.

Finding every branch point means re-sampling at every step.

Branches off branches off branches. The cost is exponential in the length of the task.

Schematic.

What we can afford: fifty independent rollouts.

Different actions at different moments, a mix of outcomes, and no two attempts share a branch point exactly.

But they share states.

Many actions and observations are near-duplicates. Cluster them by semantic similarity and every rollout becomes a route through one map of behavior states.

Every rollout strengthens the map.

Each new rollout mostly reinforces edges, tinting them toward success or failure; now and then it adds a state. The branch points surface without re-sampling.

The path already knows how it ends.

Fit a simple classifier on one task's rollouts and it predicts held-out outcomes. Successes and failures separate in the raw action–observation space and in the state map.

But that knowledge doesn't travel.

A per-task classifier needs around thirty labeled rollouts of that task, and it knows nothing about the next one.

Meet Precog-RM.

A small transformer, meta-learned across many tasks and policies, turns a rollout or any prefix of it into one point. Nothing is fitted on a new task.

A few labeled rollouts are enough.

Their means are the pass and fail prototypes. A new rollout is scored by distance. With two labeled rollouts per class, Precog-RM matches the per-task classifiers at eight.

Look inside: the latent space, your support set

Click a rollout to select it; shift-click to move it in or out of the support set.

It generalizes.

One encoder separates rollouts of policies it never saw. Across benchmarks the transfer is partial, and recovers once the benchmark joins the training pool.

It can tell before the end.

Trained on prefixes too, it scores a rollout while it is running. Gate on confidence and the prediction becomes a decision: stop, or keep going.
