Precog-RM

Divergent Paths: Learning an Outcome-Predictive Representation of LLM Agent Trajectories

Alexander Gill, Guram Gogia, Francisco Guzmán

arXiv Code Data & checkpoints

Agentic tool-use trajectories are paths through a shared embedding space of actions and environment observations. Successful and failed rollouts of the same task separate in this space. Precog-RM meta-learns a representation in which rollout success can be predicted few-shot across unseen tasks and policy models, and uses that signal as an early-stopping controller for GRPO post-training.

How it works, in one map

The same 50 rollouts of one task, retail/103 from Tau2-Bench, carried through the whole pipeline: behavior states as stations, rollouts as trains, the encoder folding each route into a point, prototypes forming from one batch of labeled rollouts, and the controller stopping a new rollout before it finishes.

1 · Behavior-state map

Every action–observation step of every rollout is embedded, and the steps of one task are clustered into behavior states (HDBSCAN over the joint action–observation similarity, minimum cluster size 5; unassigned steps form one outlier bin). Bar height is how many rollouts sit in a state at that step, bar colour is that state's empirical success rate, and each thin ribbon is one rollout, coloured by its final outcome. Click a bar to read the action–observation pairs in that state; hover a ribbon to follow one rollout.

2 · How Precog-RM scores a rollout

The encoder maps a rollout (or any prefix of it) to a 128-d latent vector. A small support set of rollouts with known outcomes defines two prototypes, the mean latent of the passing and of the failing support rollouts; every other rollout is scored by its distance to the two. The map is a 2-D PCA view of that latent space fitted on this task. Click a rollout to select it, shift-click to move it in or out of the support set, and drag the prefix slider to watch the selected rollout's latent move as the agent acts.

Scores under this support set

Queries only (support rollouts excluded). AUROC is over these queries. Scores are softmax over negative prototype distances, the rule the encoder was trained with.
